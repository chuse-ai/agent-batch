# Batches read their members, and their breaker counts ends

A Batch's row keeps only what its members cannot tell: its labels and failure breaker, who paused it and when, when it was cancelled, the breaker's counts, and what its deleted members left behind (how many, and their totals as they were deleted). Its counts by status and its cost are read from its members' rows each time it is read, so the five paths that change a job (submission, controls, a Runner's reports, a Runner's removal, deletion) keep nothing on the Batch in step; only a member's deletion, which removes the row the Batch reads, adds to the Batch's row. Its status is derived: `cancelled` once it was cancelled and every member has ended, `paused` while it is paused, `ended` once every member has ended, and `inProgress` otherwise, a Batch with no members yet included, since its jobs may come over several requests. A pause is one write to the Batch's row, which placement reads: a member waits while its Batch is paused, or, outside the Canary, while any member of the Canary is unfinished, and reads `waitingFor` with the reason. The Canary is the first `canary` members submitted, flagged as they are inserted, while no member of the Canary has ended.

The breaker counts ends, not jobs: each end of a member that succeeded or failed, unless its Runner's removal failed it, since its trial began, at the Batch's creation or at the resume of a pause the breaker made. A member resumed after it failed adds a second end, which is new evidence about the content the members share, and counting ends needs no memory on the job of whether it was counted. Only a Runner's report can end a job that way, so the counts change in one place, in the transaction that applies the report, which locks the Batches in the order of their IDs. The Batch pauses once at least two counted ends failed and their share is above its tolerance: without the floor, the first end failing would pause any Batch whose tolerance is below 100%. While a caller has paused the Batch, the breaker keeps counting but does not pause it; the caller's resume applies the same rule, and leaves the Batch paused by the breaker if the counts trip it.

Locks follow one order: a Runner before its jobs, as its removal takes them and a report takes its Runner first; a Batch before its members, wherever a statement takes both. A Batch's cancel and a submission into it lock the Batch first, so they, the Batch's pause and resume, and a member's resume and deletion come one after another; a report that ends members locks their Batches with a breaker first, several in the order of their IDs, so reports of different Runners take them in turn. A member's Batch never changes, so it is found without a lock before it is locked. Jobs are locked among themselves in the order of their IDs where a statement would otherwise take several in another order: a Batch's cancel, a report that moves jobs' statuses, and a Runner's removal. Placement locks the members it takes and reads their Batches without a lock, so it never waits on a Batch: a placement under way when a pause commits may still send members, as one that committed a moment earlier would have.

## Considered options

- A running total on the Batch at each member's end, for its counts and cost: every path that ends a job would keep it, a resumed member's cost grows after its end, and the Batch would still read its unfinished members for their live cost.
- Each member's latest Usage record: Usage records are dropped by month, and a Batch can outlive them.
- A lasting membership row per member, kept after the job is deleted, so a Batch submitted again never recreates a deleted member: a second row per job for one reader, while a job submitted alone already frees its idempotency key when it is deleted.
- Counting distinct members, withdrawing a member's count when it is resumed: a resume would write to the Batch, and the count would need a record on each job of the trial it counted in.
- A tolerance with no floor, or one measured against the Canary's size: the first would pause on one early failure; the second would wait for more failures before pausing a large Canary whose first two jobs failed.
- Placement locking the Batch's row: placement holds the members it takes while it reads their Batches, and a Batch's cancel holds the Batch while it locks its members, so the two would wait for each other.
- A member before its Batch: a member a submission adds after a cancel has locked the members it found, then ended by a report that waits for the Batch, deadlocks the cancel and the report; and a member's resume that does not lock the Batch can slip past a cancel under way.

## Consequences

Reading a Batch counts its members, which costs a scan of its members' rows; reconsider at hundreds of thousands of members per Batch. Placement checks each queued member's Batch, so a large held Batch is read on every placement. A deleted member's key is free again, so submitting its Batch again creates it anew. A host that kills jobs when it runs out of memory makes them fail, which the breaker counts like any other failure.
