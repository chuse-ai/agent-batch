# Batches read their jobs, and their breaker counts each job once

A Batch's row keeps only what its jobs cannot tell: its agent, labels and failure breaker, who paused it and when, when its breaker tripped during a caller's pause, when it was cancelled, and the breaker's counts and Canary. Its counts by status and its cost are read from its jobs' rows each time it is read, so the paths that change a job (an add, controls, a Runner's reports, a Runner's removal, deletion) keep nothing on the Batch in step, and a deleted job drops out of them; its Usage records keep its cost by Batch. Its status is derived: `cancelled` once it was cancelled and every job has ended, `paused` while it is paused, and `active` otherwise. A pause is one write to the Batch's row and changes no job's status: placement reads the Batch, and a queued job not yet sent to a Runner shows what blocks it as `blockedBy`, derived the same way. A cancel is final, also for a Batch whose jobs have all ended: it always records `cancelledAt`, after which an add and a job's resume are refused, since a runnable job would undo the cancel unseen.

The agent's Input files are stored once for the Batch, in `batchInputFile`, and the Runner is sent them beside each job's own. A job's file may not share a name with one of the agent's.

The breaker counts jobs, not ends: of the jobs whose latest counted end, a success or a failure, came since the current Canary began, those that failed. A job retried after failing has the same input, so its failures are evidence about that job, not about what the jobs share. Only a Runner's report can end a job that way, so the counts grow in one place, in the transaction that applies the report, which locks the Batches with a breaker in the order of their IDs. "Since the Canary began" compares a job's Usage record ID with the latest one when the Canary began, which the Batch keeps: the IDs are the hub's, and increase, while a job's `endedAt` is its Runner's clock. A failed job's resume takes its failure back out of the counts when its latest Usage record is after that ID; the resume already locks the Batch. A Canary is a flag on each of its jobs: the jobs added first take its places in order, and a resume of the breaker's pause clears the flags of the last Canary and flags the next `canary` jobs not yet sent to a Runner, leaving the places it could not fill to the jobs added next. A failure among the flagged jobs pauses the Batch. Once all Canary places have been filled and its jobs have ended, the share of failed jobs over `maxFailurePercent` does; until then a job sent before the Canary began, still ending, is counted but does not judge the Batch in the Canary's place. While a caller has paused the Batch, the breaker keeps counting; when its rule trips meanwhile, the Batch records when, and the caller's resume starts a new Canary as a resume of the breaker's pause does. The Canary follows Resilience4j's circuit breaker, which lets a set number of trial calls through when half-open, and the share of failed jobs follows AWS Step Functions' Distributed Map, which fails a run past its `ToleratedFailurePercentage`; telling the ends since a Canary began by Usage record ID is this project's own.

Locks follow one order: a Runner before its jobs, as its removal takes them and a report takes its Runner first; a Batch before its jobs, wherever a statement takes both. A Batch's cancel, pause and resume, an add to it, and a job's resume lock the Batch first, so they come one after another; a report that ends jobs locks their Batches with a breaker first, several in the order of their IDs, so reports of different Runners take them in turn. A job's Batch never changes, so it is found without a lock before it is locked. Jobs are locked among themselves in the order of their IDs where a statement would otherwise take several in another order: a Batch's cancel, a report that moves jobs' statuses, and a Runner's removal. A job's deletion writes nothing to its Batch, so it does not lock it. Placement locks the jobs it takes and reads their Batches without a lock: a placement under way when a pause commits may still send the jobs it took, as one that committed a moment earlier would have, so a pause stops the jobs not already being sent.

## Considered options

- A running total on the Batch at each job's end, for its counts and cost: every path that ends a job would keep it, a resumed job's cost grows after its end, and the Batch would still read its unfinished jobs for their live cost.
- Keeping what deleted jobs leave behind on the Batch, their count and totals as they were deleted: the Batch would report jobs that no longer exist, and every deletion would lock and write the Batch; Usage records already keep a deleted job's cost by Batch.
- Each job's latest Usage record, for the Batch's cost: a deleted job's stays, so the Batch's counts and cost would cover different jobs; cost by Batch, deleted jobs included, is what Usage records are for.
- Counting ends rather than jobs: one job failing again and again would pause the Batch.
- A record on each job of the Canary it counted in: comparing its latest Usage record ID with the Batch's tells the same.
- A floor of two failures before the breaker judges: a Canary of one whose job failed released the rest, and a large Canary was judged on whichever jobs ended first.
- Holding a Batch's jobs as `paused` while the Batch is: every path that changes a job would keep its status in step with the Batch, and a resume of the Batch would have to tell the jobs it held from those paused by their own control.
- Copying the agent's Input files to each job: an add would write a row per shared file per job, each locking its blob as a submission does (ADR 0031).
- Placement locking the Batch's row before its jobs: it would follow the lock order, but in a second step, finding the candidates' Batches and locking them before locking the jobs, to close a window of one statement.
- A job before its Batch: a job added after a cancel has locked the jobs it found, then ended by a report that waits for the Batch, deadlocks the cancel and the report; and a job's resume that does not lock the Batch can slip past a cancel under way.

## Consequences

Reading a Batch counts its jobs, which costs a scan of its jobs' rows; reconsider at hundreds of thousands of jobs per Batch. Placement checks each queued job's Batch and Canary, so a large blocked Batch is read on every placement. A deleted job's custom ID is free again, so adding it again creates it anew. A host that kills jobs when it runs out of memory makes them fail, which the breaker counts like any other failure.
